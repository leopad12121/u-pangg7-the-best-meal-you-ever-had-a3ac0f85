# Candidates — The Best Meal You Ever Had

Judge: house judge model, pairwise, no ties. Criteria in the judge's own
order:
1. on-subject — one line naming the best meal the entrant ever had
2. evocative — a reader can taste it, smell it, or feel where it was eaten
3. memorable — a specific detail that sticks, not a generic superlative
4. funny — a turn, an understatement, or a gap between the meal and its setting
5. tight — lands without a setup it does not need

Scored /25 (5 per criterion). Gate: one line, UTF-8, 1–200 chars, not only
whitespace. A line that explains its own joke is weaker; luxury for its own
sake does not count — the best meal is rarely the most expensive one.

## 1. pho on a plastic stool — 24/25 — PICKED
A $3 bowl of pho on a plastic stool in Saigon, and every expensive dinner since has been an apology for not being it.
(117 chars. On-subject 5, evocative 5, memorable 5, funny 4, tight 5. The
price, the stool and the city put the reader on the street in nine words, and
the second half turns every fine dinner into an apology — the joke is the
reversal of what "best" usually means. Loses a point on funny: the turn is
wry rather than a laugh.)

## 2. grandmother's dumplings — 23/25
My grandmother's dumplings, folded wrong by my small hands, steamed anyway — perfection never tasted like that again.
(117 chars. On-subject 5, evocative 5, memorable 5, funny 3, tight 5. Warm and
specific, and "folded wrong" is the detail that makes it true. Loses on
funny: it is moving more than it is funny, and the judge rewards a turn.)

## 3. gas-station sandwich — 22/25
A gas-station sandwich at 2 a.m. after eighteen hours driving, and nothing Michelin ever beat it.
(99 chars. On-subject 5, evocative 4, memorable 4, funny 4, tight 4. The
2 a.m. gas-station setting does the same cheap-beats-dear work as the pick,
but "nothing Michelin ever beat it" is a blunter version of the same punch,
and the meal itself is barely described.)

## 4. the ramen I forgot to photograph — 22/25
The ramen was so good I forgot to photograph it, which is how I know it was the best meal of my life.
(103 chars. On-subject 5, evocative 3, memorable 4, funny 4, tight 4. A
modern, honest test of greatness — forgetting the photo — but the meal itself
is a blank: no taste, no place, no detail for the reader to hold.)

## 5. burnt toast, runny eggs — 22/25
Burnt toast and runny eggs my father made the morning I left home; I have ordered it everywhere since and never found it.
(119 chars. On-subject 5, evocative 4, memorable 5, funny 3, tight 5. The
last clause is quietly devastating. Loses on funny and evocative: the meal is
ordinary on purpose, but the line leans on feeling rather than flavour.)

## 6. a single peach — 21/25
The meal was a single peach, warm from the tree, juice down my chin — I ate it like it owed me money.
(102 chars. On-subject 4, evocative 5, memorable 4, funny 4, tight 4. The
peach is vivid and the last clause is the funniest image in the set. Docked
on-subject: a peach strains the word "meal", and a judge may read it as
dodging the brief.)

## 7. airport sushi — 21/25
Airport sushi, technically a war crime, eaten on the floor of Terminal 4 — still the best thing I ever tasted, and I refuse to explain why.
(136 chars. On-subject 5, evocative 4, memorable 4, funny 4, tight 4. "I
refuse to explain why" is a good deadpan close. Docked on tight (136 chars,
three clauses) and evocative: the sushi itself is never tasted, only the
setting.)

## 8. the meal I cooked wrong — 20/25
I cooked it myself, got everything slightly wrong, and it was still the best meal I ever had — because it was mine.
(114 chars. On-subject 5, evocative 3, memorable 3, funny 3, tight 4. Honest
and warm, but the meal is undescribed and the last clause explains its own
sentiment, which the brief warns against.)

## Revision of the winner

Draft: "A $3 bowl of pho on a plastic stool in Saigon, and every expensive dinner since has been an apology for not being it." (117 chars)
Revised: "A $3 bowl of pho on a plastic stool in Saigon; every expensive dinner since has been an apology for not being it." (113 chars)

The "and" came out and a semicolon took its place: the two halves now sit
side by side as a flat statement of fact instead of a story, which lets the
reversal land harder. Four characters shorter (helps the size tie-break:
exact ties go to the smaller text). `main.py` was written then edited
(thinking_revised: 2 writes in this session).
